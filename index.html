      §01 · A grammar machine for authorised research

      Enigmista.

      Enigmista models the structure of an authorised password corpus and generates new, previously-unseen candidates that fit its distribution — for policy audits, honeyword decoys, synthetic data, and research.

      enigmista (it.) — one who crafts and solves enigmas

      §02 · Structure

      It reads the shape of a corpus, never the secrets of a person.

      Give it a seed corpus you're authorised to analyse — a population of passwords — and it learns the grammar underneath: the templates people reach for, the slots they fill, the themes they draw from.

      Neural · the language model
      Induces the grammar from the seed — names the themes, categorises the slots, and extends the dictionaries with plausible members it was never shown.
      Symbolic · sampler & scorer
      A deterministic sampler draws candidates in probability order; a scorer checks them against real passwords held out from training.

      The model proposes the grammar. The symbolic layer owns the probability.

      §03 · Uses

      Three uses. All defensive.

      • ⟨audit⟩

        Password-policy audits

        Test a policy you're authorised to audit against the realistic structure of the passwords it governs.

      • ⟨decoy⟩

        Honeywords & decoys

        Decoys that fit the corpus's distribution. The more plausible the decoy, the more reliably a login with it signals a breach.

      • ⟨synth⟩

        Synthetic data & research

        Password sets with the statistical shape of the real thing — for testing systems and studying how people choose.

      §04 · Objective

      Predict the passwords you haven't seen.

      A model that perfectly memorises its seed is worthless — it only regenerates what it was given. Enigmista is judged on a frozen held-out split it never trained on: on how much of the unseen-but-real it manages to predict.

      Held-out — never shown to the model. Lights up when a sample lands on it.

      §05 · Method

      A loop that only believes its scorer.

      induce → sample → score → analyse misses → refine — repeated until coverage plateaus or hits a target.

      1. 01 · Corpus & protocol

        Load the authorised seed, deduplicate, and freeze an 80/20 train / held-out split — once. Never re-split; that leaks the test set. Markov and basic-PCFG baselines set the number to beat.

      2. 02 · Induce

        The model builds a probabilistic context-free grammar from the training split: templates, slots, terminal dictionaries, probabilities, named themes. On a frontier model, an enrichment pass adds plausible unseen terms.

      3. 03 · Sample

        Draw a budget of candidates at temperature τ, ordered by probability — the guess order. Low τ clusters on the modes; high τ reaches into the tails.

      4. 04 · Score

        The bundled scorer runs the candidates against the held-out split. Coverage, novelty, and the delta over baseline come only from the scorer — never self-assessed.

      5. 05 · Analyse misses & refine

        Read the held-out passwords the candidates missed, hypothesise the structure that's missing, refine the grammar, and go round again.

      6. 06 · Stop

        When coverage beats the best baseline by a clear margin and gains plateau — or the iteration cap is reached, or coverage regresses twice and the best grammar is handed back.

              §07 · Scope

              The corpus boundary is the whole line.

              The same machinery is defensive on a corpus and an attack on a person. Modelling how a population chooses passwords helps you harden policy, plant decoys, and build test data. Pointing it at one individual turns distribution-modelling into an attack on them. Enigmista stays on one side of that line by design.

              In scope

              • Password-policy audits the operator is authorised to run.
              • Honeyword and decoy generation.
              • Synthetic-data creation and research.
              • Only from a seed corpus the operator supplies and is authorised to use — a population, not one person.

              Out of scope — refused

              • Using a specific person's details — a name, a birthday, a pet's name — to guess their passwords.
              • Producing or ranking candidates aimed at an individual's accounts.
              • Treating one person's passwords as if they were a corpus.

              Asked to cross the line, Enigmista names the boundary, offers the legitimate version — model a corpus you're authorised to use — and stops. Candidate lists and grammars are treated as sensitive: written to a local ./out/, never anywhere shared or public.

              This site keeps the same rule: a bundled toy list, no field to type a password into, no analytics, no trackers.

              §08 · Install

              Run the real loop.

              Enigmista ships as a skill. The loop needs a runtime with a disk and script execution: Claude Code, Gemini CLI, or a local model on Hermes. It hands back at its checkpoints — baselines first, then the first grammar, then coverage against baseline after every iteration.
