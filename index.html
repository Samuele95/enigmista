§01 · A grammar machine for authorised research

Enigmista.

Enigmista models the structure of an authorised password corpus and generates new, previously-unseen candidates that fit its distribution — for policy audits, honeyword decoys, synthetic data, and research.

enigmista (it.) — one who crafts and solves enigmas

§02 · What it is

It reads the shape of a corpus, never the secrets of a person.

Enigmista is a neurosymbolic, agentic password-candidate generator. You give it a seed corpus you are authorised to analyse — a population of passwords — and it learns the grammar underneath: the templates people reach for, the slots they fill, the themes they draw from. Then it writes new candidates in that grammar, in the order the grammar thinks most likely.

Two layers do the work, and each does only what it is good at.

Neural The language model
Induces a generative grammar from the seed: names the themes, categorises the slots, and extends the dictionaries with plausible members it was never shown. This is where it beats a plain statistical model — it generalises beyond what it saw.
Symbolic The sampler and the scorer
A deterministic sampler draws candidates from that grammar, ordered by probability. A scorer measures how many passwords from a held-out split — real ones the model never trained on — those candidates actually cover. The model proposes the grammar; the symbolic layer owns the probability.

Three uses, all defensive

  • ⟨audit⟩

    Password-policy audits

    Test a policy you are authorised to audit against the realistic structure of the passwords it governs — the templates, themes, and digit habits a real population actually uses.

  • ⟨decoy⟩

    Honeywords and decoys

    Generate decoys that fit the corpus's distribution. The more plausible a decoy, the more reliably a login that uses one signals a breach.

  • ⟨synth⟩

    Synthetic data

    Produce password sets with the statistical shape of the real thing, for testing systems and studying structure — and for research into how people actually choose passwords.

§03 · The method

Predict the passwords you haven't seen.

A model that perfectly memorises its seed corpus is worthless: it just regenerates what it was already given. So Enigmista is never judged on the seed. It is judged on a frozen held-out split it never trained on — on how much of the unseen-but-real it manages to predict. That reframing is the whole tool.

It runs as a goal-based loop: induce → sample → score → analyse misses → refine, repeated until it plateaus or hits a target.

  1. 01 · Corpus & protocol

    Load the authorised seed, deduplicate, and freeze an 80/20 train / held-out split — once. It is never re-split; that would leak the test set. Markov and basic-PCFG baselines set the number to beat.

  2. 02 · Induce

    The model builds a first probabilistic context-free grammar from the training split: templates, slot categories, terminal dictionaries, production probabilities, named themes. On a frontier model, an enrichment pass extends the dictionaries with plausible unseen terms.

  3. 03 · Sample

    Draw a budget of candidates at temperature τ, ordered by probability — the guess order. Low τ clusters on the modes; high τ reaches into the tails.

  4. 04 · Score

    The bundled scorer runs the candidates against the held-out split. Coverage, novelty, and the delta over baseline come only from the scorer — never self-assessed.

  5. 05 · Analyse misses & refine

    Read the held-out passwords the candidates missed, hypothesise the structure that's missing, refine the grammar, and go round again.

  6. 06 · Stop

    When coverage beats the best baseline by a clear margin and the gains plateau — or when the iteration cap is reached, or coverage regresses twice running and the best grammar is handed back.

Verification is non-negotiable. A run never claims an improvement because an edit succeeded or the candidates look plausible — it quotes the scorer's numbers on the frozen held-out set.

          §05 · Scope

          The corpus boundary is the whole line.

          The same machinery is defensive on a corpus and an attack on a person. Modelling how a population chooses passwords helps you harden policy, plant decoys, and build test data. Pointing that machinery at one individual turns distribution-modelling into an attack on them. Enigmista stays on one side of that line by design.

          In scope

          • Password-policy audits the operator is authorised to run.
          • Honeyword and decoy generation.
          • Synthetic-data creation and research.
          • Only from a seed corpus the operator supplies and is authorised to use — a population of passwords, not one person's.

          Out of scope — refused

          • Using a specific person's details — a name, a birthday, a pet's name — to guess their passwords.
          • Producing or ranking candidates aimed at an individual's accounts.
          • Treating one person's passwords as if they were a corpus.

          Asked to cross the line, Enigmista names the boundary, offers the legitimate version — model a corpus you're authorised to use — and stops. Its artifacts, candidate lists and grammars, are treated as sensitive: written to a local ./out/, never anywhere shared or public.

          This site keeps the same rule. The sandbox uses a bundled toy list, there is no field to type a password into, and there are no analytics or trackers.

          §06 · Install

          Run the real loop.

          Enigmista ships as a skill. The loop needs a runtime with a disk and script execution: Claude Code, Gemini CLI, or a local model on Hermes. It hands back at its checkpoints — baselines first, then the first grammar, then coverage against baseline after every iteration.
